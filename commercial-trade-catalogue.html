Trade product catalogue

A trade product catalogue and stockist finder, where shoppers find a nearby stockist, prices show in pounds or euros, and stockists can request changes to their own listing.

Sector
Trade tools and accessories
My role
Developed from supplied designs: finder, profile form, approval queue and price display
Built with
WordPress, ACF, FacetWP with maps and proximity search, Google Geocoding API, Gravity Forms, SCSS and Gulp
Scale
33 blocks, with templates for products, stockists, brands, guides and videos
AT A GLANCE
  • Content: Stockists (with locations), products (with brands and trades), “what’s hot”, guides and videos
  • Search: FacetWP map and list, location autocomplete, filter by trade
  • Forms: A three-step Gravity Form for premium stockist profiles
  • Prices: Inc and ex VAT, promotional and RRP, pounds and euros

1 · What it needed

What the site had to do.

The brand sells through stockists rather than direct. The job was to help someone find a stockist near them or one that suits their trade, to present products and brands, and to keep stockist information accurate without the team retyping everything.

Premium stockists get a fuller profile (an advert card and a profile page), and customers in the UK and Ireland both needed prices they could read.

2 · What a visitor can do

The site in use.

  • Type a town or postcode, with autocomplete limited to the UK and Ireland, and see stockists nearest first on a map and in a list with contact details, directions and a “more” link.
  • Filter stockists by trade, or browse by region, and open a stockist’s own page.
  • Browse products by brand and trade, with prices shown including or excluding VAT, and switch between pounds and euros.
  • Read buying guides and watch videos from the resource pages, and see a “what’s hot” slider.

3 · What I built

What I built, and how.

The stockist finder

Stockists are a custom post type with a location taxonomy, searched with FacetWP using a map, a town or postcode field and a filter by trade. On the finder landing page, the archive and region pages, results default to sorting by distance, so a search shows the nearest first without the visitor choosing. Clicking a marker glides the map to it and nudges the information window further on mobile so it is not hidden. I also replaced the default pagination with my own pager so it matches the design.

Geocoding stockists in bulk

Stockists arrive with addresses but not always coordinates. A helper turns an address into latitude and longitude with the Google Geocoding API, and an admin-triggered routine fills in any stockist whose map coordinates are missing or zero, leaving the rest untouched.

Self-service profile updates

Premium stockists update their profile through a three-step Gravity Form. Step one identifies the business by its trade account number, checked over AJAX with a nonce. The theme then loads the stockist’s current text, links, opening times and images into the form, so they edit rather than retype. The submission dates and guidance (including a PDF) come from admin options, so the team can change deadlines without a developer. The account number selects which listing an update is for. It does not make anything public.

A review queue that only stores what changed

On submission, the theme compares each answer with the live value and stores only the fields that differ, as pending values beside the live fields, along with who submitted them and when. A table on the stockist’s admin screen shows current and pending values side by side, with images as thumbnails, and offers Approve All, Approve Selected and Reject All. Approving a new image brings the uploaded file into the media library; approving text updates the live field. Until then, the public listing is unchanged.

Prices in two currencies and two VAT states

A reusable price block shows inc-VAT and ex-VAT figures, with promotional and RRP prices, and a pounds and euros switch. Where an editor has entered a euro price, it is used. Otherwise the price is converted in the browser at a rate fetched from a public exchange-rate service, with a fallback rate if that call fails, and the visitor’s choice is remembered for a day. A separate rule changes the VAT label for products that carry none.

A catalogue with several ways in

Products have a brand taxonomy and trade categories, there is a separate “what’s hot” content type with its own slider, and guides and videos have their own listing templates. The brand archive is shown in random order, seeded so it holds steady for a few minutes while someone pages through, instead of repeating brands between pages.

4 · Decisions

Choices worth explaining.

Stage changes instead of publishing them

Problem

Stockists should be able to correct their own details, but the public listing should not change without a check.

Approach

Submitted values are stored as pending metadata next to the live fields, and only differing fields are recorded.

Trade-off

Someone has to review the queue.

Pre-fill the form from the live listing

Problem

A blank form invites people to retype everything and to introduce errors.

Approach

The account number loads the current values first, so the stockist edits rather than starts again.

Trade-off

The form only works once the stockist has their account number to hand.

Convert currency in the browser

Problem

Euro prices needed to be readable without a second price list for every product.

Approach

Explicit euro prices win. Otherwise the page converts the pound price at the day’s rate and falls back to a fixed rate if the service is down.

Trade-off

Converted figures are indicative, not a quoted euro price.
