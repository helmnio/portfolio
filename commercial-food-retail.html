A WooCommerce shop for food and merchandise, built alongside the story of a food market business, its traders and its events.

1 · What it needed

What the site had to do.

The site had two jobs: tell the story of a well-known market business and its traders, and sell merchandise and pantry products. The shop had to feel part of the brand rather than a default store, and editors needed to link the two.

2 · What a visitor can do

The site in use.

  • Browse the shop with a category filter, then open a product.
  • On a product, flick through a gallery, choose a colour or size, see the price and whether it is in stock, set a quantity and add it to the bag.
  • See the bag count in the header update without a page reload.
  • Read the story, meet the traders (each has a page and gallery), try recipes and see upcoming events.

3 · What I built

What I built, and how.

A shop that matches the brand

The theme provides its own WooCommerce shop and single product templates, built on WooCommerce’s standard behaviour for variations, stock and add to cart. The default product, shop-loop and wrapper actions are removed so the theme controls the markup, and a thank-you message is added after the order. The bag count in the header updates through a WooCommerce cart fragment.

Product pages that respond to the product

A gallery with a main slider, thumbnails and a lightbox uses the product’s own images. Colour and size choices are built from the product’s attributes and start on the product’s default values, and the size picker recognises several differently named size attributes. The quantity control is a stepper limited by the product’s maximum purchase quantity, and the page shows stock status clearly.

Story, market and residents

The content side is a set of blocks for the market’s story, a timeline, careers, recipes, sign-ups, a pop-up, upcoming events and a residents listing, where each resident has its own page and gallery. Heroes vary by section, including story, residents and careers.

Featured products inside content

A feature-products block lets editors choose products and place them inside story, article and resident pages. Each one is rendered with WooCommerce’s own product template, so the shop and the editorial content can lead into each other.

4 · Why this approach

Why I built it this way.

Override templates, keep WooCommerce behaviour. Replace the templates and attach to WooCommerce’s own behaviour, leaving cart, checkout and payment logic as they are. The default shop layout would not match the brand, but rebuilding commerce logic would be risky. Removing the default hooks is blunt. WooCommerce or plugin updates that add to them will not show up until I check the overrides.
