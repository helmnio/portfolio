1 · What it needed

What the site had to do.

Customers browse vehicles by model and by what is in stock. The company has its own showrooms and a nationwide network of stockists, who need to add their own vehicles and locations. The company needs to stay in control of what appears on the site.

2 · What a visitor can do

The site in use.

  • Browse the range by model, with model pages that include specification tables, finance information and upgrade options.
  • Search current stock by wheelbase and price from a form that appears on several pages.
  • Type a location or postcode, or choose “Nationwide”, and see stockists on a map or as a list, with the main showrooms listed separately.
  • Read buying guides and search them.

3 · What I built

What I built, and how.

Models, stock and locations

Vehicles, models and locations are separate content types with their own templates and a library of blocks: model listings, model tables, sliders, finance information and upgrade options. A latest-stock page is filtered with FacetWP, and locations use FacetWP’s map with proximity.

Search forms built from the data

The stock search block builds its options from the data instead of a fixed list: the wheelbase dropdown comes from the values in use, and the price range runs from the lowest to the highest price in stock, rounded to the nearest £10,000. It posts to the stock page as a normal GET request, so results can be linked and bookmarked.

Minimum and maximum dropdowns

FacetWP’s number-range filters are text boxes by default. For price, mileage and year, I replaced them with minimum and maximum selectors whose options come from the lowest and highest indexed values, in sensible steps (£10,000, 5,000 miles, one year). The selections are copied back into the filter and restored after each AJAX refresh, so they do not reset.

A map that behaves

When a location is searched, results keep their distance order. The farthest radius option is relabelled “Nationwide” rather than a number of miles. Main showrooms and other stockists have different map markers, so the visitor can tell them apart.

A dealer workflow

Dealers get a Dealer role that starts from the Editor role. Anything a dealer creates in the vehicle or location types is forced to pending, including an attempt to publish an existing post, and every administrator is emailed with a link when a post goes pending. What dealers see is trimmed: the admin menu shows only vehicles, the list shows only their own vehicles, and the toolbar is cut to a log-out link. What they can publish is controlled separately by the forced pending status.

Guides with their own address and search

Buying guides have their own permalink structure and rewrite rules, listing and card blocks, and an AJAX search. The custom search page is set to noindex so it does not appear in search engines, and breadcrumbs insert “Guides” or “Models” so the trail makes sense.

4 · Decisions

Choices worth explaining.

Let dealers add stock, but keep publishing central

Problem

A nationwide network needs to enter its own vehicles, but a dealer should not be able to put something live unchecked.

Approach

A role with editor-level capabilities, combined with a filter that forces pending status and an admin notification.

Trade-off

The admin team becomes the approval bottleneck.

Build the search from the data

Problem

Hard-coded wheelbase and price options go out of date as stock changes.

Approach

The block reads the values from the vehicle fields each time.

Trade-off

It runs a few queries on each page where the block appears.
