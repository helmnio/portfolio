1 · What it needed

What the site had to do.

A holiday business with several parks needs each park to present its own accommodation, offers and things to do, while the whole site still behaves like one brand. It also needed to let visitors check availability, promote homes for sale and recruit staff.

2 · What a visitor can do

The site in use.

  • Start a search from the booking block on the homepage, or choose a park or accommodation type.
  • Filter accommodation by park, type and facilities, with the cheapest first by default.
  • Open an accommodation page, see its gallery, price per night and amenities, and check availability in a calendar for that park.
  • Browse offers and homes for sale, and enquire about a specific home.

3 · What I built

What I built, and how.

Accommodation with filters

Accommodation is a custom post type with taxonomies for type, facilities and park, so a visitor can narrow the list by where they want to stay or what is included. FacetWP drives the filtering, with separate templates for the archive, single accommodation and each taxonomy. Listings sort by price from low to high by default on every page except the blog and offers, where that order makes no sense, controlled by a short list of exceptions.

Availability on the page

Each accommodation page loads an availability calendar from the third-party booking system, picked by the accommodation’s park. A product ID field ties the page to the booking record, and a switch hides the calendar for products that cannot be booked online. “Check availability” on the page jumps straight to it.

A page system for several parks

The theme includes park-specific accommodation templates, built from the same blocks: heroes, icon strips, costs, awards, “things to do” sliders, FAQs, maps and booking calls to action. That keeps the parks consistent while letting each one feel specific.

Offers, homes for sale and jobs

Offers, jobs and homes for sale are their own content types. Homes for sale have location and type taxonomies and a listing template. A custom form tag and shortcode add the listing’s ID, title and path to the enquiry as a hidden value, so the team knows exactly which home an enquiry is about.

4 · Why this approach

Why I built it this way.

Templates per park, blocks for everything else. Page templates define the structure and ACF blocks supply the content. Each park needs its own page without every page becoming a bespoke build. There are several templates to keep in step.

Carry the listing into the enquiry. A small Contact Form 7 form tag fills a hidden field from the current post. An enquiry that does not say which home it is about costs the team a follow-up question. It only works on pages that are single posts.
