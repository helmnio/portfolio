1 · What it needed

What the site had to do.

An estate agency lives on three things: people finding a property, trusting the agency enough to get in touch, and the agency capturing who they are. The site needed searchable sales and lettings, guides that build credibility, and tools that give visitors something useful in return for their details.

2 · What a visitor can do

The site in use.

  • Search properties by place and a radius from one mile to 250, then narrow by property type (each with a count), bedrooms, how recently they were added and price.
  • Tick “include under offer or sold STC” to see homes that have gone, otherwise they are hidden.
  • Load more results without leaving the page, and open a property with its gallery, map and floor plans.
  • Use a mortgage, stamp duty or rental yield calculator that asks for contact details and produces a result.
  • Read area guides and neighbourhood pages.

3 · What I built

What I built, and how.

Property search and listings

Properties are a custom post type with a department field (for example residential sales) and are filtered with FacetWP across a set of dedicated filter blocks for sales, lettings and tabbed searches. There are templates for the property archive, single property, neighbourhoods and team members. Search links can open already filtered, and I dealt with Google Maps being loaded twice by removing the duplicate loader and controlling when FacetWP loads it.

Defaults that match expectations

The sales search opens with “for sale” pre-selected, so sold-subject-to-contract homes are hidden until the visitor asks for them. The lettings search does the same for let-agreed properties. Property cards show a status badge for Sold STC, Under Offer, Let Agreed and Let, and the archive pages six at a time. When a place is searched, results keep their distance order.

Load more, without a second set of markup

Recently reduced properties and the guides use “load more” buttons backed by AJAX. Each request is checked with a nonce, its inputs are sanitised, and the cards come from the same render function used on the first page load, so loaded items look identical and there is one place to change a card.

Calculators that become leads

The mortgage, rental yield and stamp duty calculators post the visitor’s details and figures to AJAX handlers. Each handler checks the required fields, sanitises them and submits a Gravity Forms entry through GFAPI. That way the enquiry arrives in the same place, with the same notifications, as every other form on the site, and includes the numbers they calculated.

Content blocks for the marketing team

The rest of the build is a library of ACF blocks: video and image heroes, property listings, a team slider, tour and call-to-action banners, FAQs, testimonials, an Instagram feed and recruitment content, so the team can assemble pages without a developer.

4 · Decisions

Choices worth explaining.

Calculators submit real form entries

Problem

A calculator that only shows a number gives the agency nothing to follow up.

Approach

The handlers call GFAPI::submit_form, so each use arrives as a normal Gravity Forms entry and triggers the usual notifications.

Trade-off

The calculator depends on the form IDs staying in step with the theme.

One card renderer for first load and load more

Problem

Cards built twice, once in the template and once in the AJAX response, drift apart.

Approach

Both paths call the same function.
